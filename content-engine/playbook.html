1Strategy: Where the Money Is

The niche and platform decisions below follow from 2025–2026 monetization data, not taste. Two facts drive everything: payouts differ ~30x by platform and niche, and YouTube's July 2025 policy update decides what stays monetizable.

1.1Platform economics

WhereTypical payout per 1,000 views
YouTube long-form — personal finance / investing~$10–25 RPM (CPMs $15–50)
YouTube long-form — real estate~$15–30 RPM
YouTube long-form — tech / AI / software~$20–40 RPM
YouTube long-form — general entertainment~$2–5 RPM

InstagramNo meaningful per-view program; sponsors / affiliate only

Consequences: YouTube long-form is the only real ad-revenue engine — TikTok and Instagram are distribution and funnel, not income. US/UK viewers pay 3–5x other regions, so topics should skew American.

1.2Niche verdicts

Sports news Dropped

Weakest on every axis: leagues (NFL, Premier League) run aggressive Content ID that claims broadcast footage nearly instantly; footage-free sports talk lives on personality, which AI can't fake well; CPMs are mid-tier; and news has zero long-tail — a video about last night's game earns for 48 hours, then dies.

Finance / real estate Channel #1

Highest RPMs on the platform, and ads are the smaller prize: fintech affiliate deals (brokerages, cards, real estate platforms) pay $50–100+ per conversion. The fix: don't make it news — news decays, explainers compound. Run ~70% evergreen explainers / 30% news-reactive. Caution: finance is YMYL content, so every number needs a human fact-check.

Conspiracy / ancient history Reframed → Channel #2

Conspiracy-framed channels carry only ~16% of the ad volume of mainstream channels — advertisers exclude them. The same material framed as ancient mysteries / history documentary (evidence-first, open questions rather than cover-ups) monetizes fine, holds excellent watch time, and is fully evergreen and faceless-native. Keep the topic; kill the word "conspiracy" and all "they're hiding it" framing.

1.3Reserve niches (expansion after day 90)

  • Business collapse / scam documentaries — $8–18 RPM, evergreen forever, faceless-native; the best effort-to-income ratio in the faceless space. (The finance channel's "Money History" bucket seeds this.)
  • Macro-economics & geopolitics explainers — high CPM, script-driven, AI-friendly.
  • AI / tech tools & news — $20–40 RPM plus recurring SaaS affiliate commissions.
  • Micro-health / curiosity science — dramatically underserved with viral ceilings in the millions; stay clear of medical advice.

1.4The constraint that shapes everything

In July 2025, YouTube renamed its repetitious-content rule the "inauthentic content" policy, explicitly to deny monetization to mass-produced AI output — templated slideshows with synthetic narration are the named target. Channels that keep monetization use AI as the production tool while the output stays original and transformative: unique research, real scripting, a distinctive voice and visual identity, and a human pass on every video.

The pyramid model

Don't publish 30 separate daily productions. Produce 5–6 quality long-form videos per week across two channels (the revenue engine), and atomize each into 3–5 Shorts / TikToks / Reels per day plus graphics (the growth engine — where daily volume is fine and expected). Two or three focused channels beat ten template channels that all get caught in the same policy filter.

1.5The differentiation moat

Bad production doesn't kill faceless channels — being generic does. There are hundreds of finance explainers and mystery channels; a viewer needs a reason to subscribe to this one rather than let the algorithm serve them whatever's next. The moat is a signature format viewers can name, repeated in every video:

  • Finance — "The Ledger." Every video ends on the same branded dashboard: the three numbers that matter for that topic (housing: inventory, price-to-income, rent-vs-buy), rendered in the channel's navy/gold chart style. The Ledger is the screenshot people share, the segment viewers wait for, and the reason to subscribe: the channel is a system for reading the economy, not a feed of takes. Act structure is forensic — follow the money from the viewer's pocket to whoever profits.
  • Mysteries — "The Ladder." Every video's final act runs the branded epistemic ladder — WE KNOW / RESEARCHERS THINK / SOME SPECULATE — with a recurring three-rung graphic and sound cue. The Ladder is the channel's promise: wonder without being lied to. In a niche full of credulity, honesty is the differentiation.

Signature segments do triple duty: they serialize the channel (binge and subscribe behavior), they build brand recall, and they are standing evidence of originality under the inauthentic-content policy. They also make packaging consistent — The Ledger and The Ladder have fixed visual treatments (Section 5).

4The Execution Plan

Long-form YouTube is the revenue engine; everything vertical is the growth engine derived from it. Built for a solo operator at ~25–30 hours/week and a lean tool budget.

4.1Phase 0 — Setup (Week 1)

  1. Create two YouTube channels (one per niche), plus matching TikTok, Instagram, and (for finance) Pinterest accounts. Same handle everywhere.
  2. Lock brand identity per channel using the Section 5 style guide: one ElevenLabs voice, one visual style, one thumbnail template, one intro sting — and the two signature segments, The Ledger and The Ladder (Section 1.5). Consistency is what separates "a channel" from "AI slop" — for both the algorithm and policy reviewers.
  3. Write 10 scripts before publishing anything (channel prompt → punch-up → human pass). A banked buffer is what makes daily posting survivable.
  4. Set up a Notion (or Sheets) board: idea bank (Sections 2–3), script status, source lists, publish calendar, per-video stats.

4.4Publishing schedule

Long-form (the revenue engine)

  • Finance: Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday — published 2–4pm ET.
  • Mysteries: Monday, Wednesday, Friday. (Solo? Start at 2/week — Wednesday + Sunday — and add the third when the pipeline is smooth.)
  • On YouTube, consistency beats clock-time: the algorithm serves impressions over days, not minutes. Never miss a slot.

Short-form (the growth engine) — 2 posts/day per brand

  • TikTok: 6–8pm (strongest 2026 window; Sunday/Monday best, avoid Fri/Sat). Make at least one daily TikTok over 60 seconds so it is Creator Rewards-eligible.
  • Instagram Reels: 7–9pm weekdays.
  • YouTube Shorts: 12–3pm.
  • Upload natively to each platform — no watermarks (TikTok watermarks suppress Reels reach). Stagger; don't cross-post simultaneously.

4.5The repurposing map — every long-form becomes 8–10 assets

  1. 3–5 vertical clips (extraction prompt in 6.6 plans them; Opus Clip cuts them; you rewrite each hook — the first line of a clip is 80% of its performance).
  2. One 60-second+ TikTok cut (Rewards-eligible) plus shorter teasers ending on a cliffhanger that points to the long-form.
  3. YouTube Shorts versions with a pinned comment linking the full video.
  4. One Instagram carousel: finance = The Ledger as a swipe-through infographic; mystery = the story in six images (the Midjourney art already exists).
  5. A YouTube Community post (poll: "which mystery next?" — free engagement signal).
  6. Pinterest pins of finance infographics (real search traffic, zero extra work).

Why this satisfies both goals

"Multiple pieces per day, every day" is generated from 5–6 long-form productions per week — not 30. Daily volume where it's expected (short-form), original transformative work where it's policed (long-form).

4.890-day scorecard

  • Targets: thumbnail CTR 4%+ · average view duration 40%+ · Shorts 30-second retention 70%+.
  • Kill rule: any format under 2% CTR and 25% AVD after 8 attempts gets retired.
  • Scale rule: any video doing 5x+ the channel median becomes a series immediately — this, not the initial plan, is how the channel finds its shape.
  • Day-90 decision: double down on whichever channel is clearly winning. The idea banks hold ~4 months of long-form content per channel, so you'll know before you run dry.
  • Quarterly: re-read the Section 5 style guide against the top 10% of videos and update it — the style guide follows the data, not the other way around.

5Content Style Guide

Lock this before video #1. A consistent look, sound, and packaging system is what makes two-person-months of AI output read as a brand — to viewers, to the algorithm, and to policy reviewers. Every rule here exists to be executed fast and repeated forever.

5.1Finance — visual identity

  • Palette: deep navy base, gold accent, off-white text. Every chart, lower-third, and end-card uses it. (One palette = instant recognition in the feed.)
  • Typography on screen: one bold grotesque sans (Archivo, Inter, or similar) for lower-thirds and stat callouts; numbers rendered oversized — the number IS the visual.
  • Charts: dark-background charts, the one series that matters highlighted in gold, everything else gray. One reveal per chart (the line continuing past "today," the bar that dwarfs the rest). Build in Datawrapper/Flourish with a saved channel theme.
  • B-roll: stock graded slightly desaturated and cool. Avoid clichés (handshakes, skylines, coin jars); prefer close-ups of objects with stakes — keys, receipts, meters, contracts.
  • Motion rules: cut every 3–5 seconds; a chart may hold up to 8 seconds while the VO works; stills get a 103–106% push. Nothing sits still longer than the sentence about it.
  • The Ledger end-card: fixed three-number dashboard layout, same grid every video.

5.2Mysteries — visual identity

  • Palette: night slate and stone grays lit by ochre/torchlight gold. One grade, applied to everything.
  • Art direction: painterly realism, NOT photorealism — it's more beautiful, it sidesteps synthetic-media disclosure gray zones, and it hides AI artifacts. Signature move: dramatic scale contrast — tiny human figures against monumental stone.
  • Maps: Google Earth Studio orbital pushes with a consistent label style; every video geolocates its site in Act 1.
  • Motion rules: slow Ken Burns 103–108%; art holds 6–10 seconds; one deliberate "hold on silence" moment per act (the pattern interrupt that brands the channel).
  • Typography on screen: a display serif (Cormorant, Playfair) for titles; small-caps place/date stamps as a series motif: "GÖBEKLI TEPE — c. 9500 BCE".
  • The Ladder graphic: fixed three-rung motif (WE KNOW / RESEARCHERS THINK / SOME SPECULATE) with its own sound cue.

Keep the style string identical across every image in a video — and across videos. Consistency is the brand; the {SUBJECT} slot is the only thing that changes.

5.3Thumbnail system

Rules for both channels:

  • One focal subject. If a thumbnail needs explaining, it fails.
  • Text: 2–4 words maximum, and the words must NOT repeat the title — thumbnail and title are two different pieces of information (title = the what; thumbnail = the why-care).
  • Readable at 120 pixels wide. Zoom out and squint before exporting.
  • Three variants per video into YouTube's Test & Compare; note the winning pattern monthly and feed it back into the template.
  • Finance template: navy field; one bold object or chart motif center-right; white grotesque text top-left with a gold underline bar. Example — title "Why Nobody Can Afford a House Anymore"; thumbnail text "6X YOUR SALARY" over a house-shaped chart line.
  • Mystery template: dark scene, single warm light source, scale contrast; gold serif text at bottom; a small corner "epoch stamp" (e.g., "9500 BCE") as the series marker. Example — title "Göbekli Tepe: The Temple That Rewrote Human History"; thumbnail text "BURIED ON PURPOSE".

5.4Title formulas

  • The colon pivot (mystery default): {Proper noun}: The {Category} That {Twist} — "Göbekli Tepe: The Temple That Rewrote Human History".
  • Negative space (curiosity default): "Why Nobody…", "The {X} No One Can {Verb}".
  • The number anchor: a concrete number where honest — "$37 Trillion", "70-Ton", "5,300-Year-Old".
  • The honest question: only when genuinely open — matches the debunk formats.

Rules: under 60 characters; front-load the searchable noun; never promise what the video doesn't cash (retention collapse is the algorithmic death penalty); the title states the what, the thumbnail supplies the why-care.

5.6Sound identity

  • One intro sting under 2 seconds, identical every video — the audio logo.
  • Finance bed: minimal electronic pulse, 90–110 BPM, no melodic hook competing with the VO.
  • Mystery bed: low drones, sparse percussion, one recurring motif.
  • Reuse the same 2–3 licensed tracks per channel so the channel sounds like itself; duck music 12–15 dB under the voice.
  • The branded silence: music cuts to nothing on the final thesis line — and on each channel's signature moment (The Ledger reveal; The Ladder's first rung).

5.7Short-form presentation spec

  • 9:16, 1080×1920. Keep text and faces inside the middle ~70% — the top ~15% and bottom ~20% are platform UI zones.
  • Captions always on: 3–5 words per line, keyword emphasized in the channel accent color.
  • The hook text overlay mirrors the spoken hook for the first 2 seconds.
  • For loop endings, trim the final beat so the last frame flows into the first.
  • Channel grade and fonts carry over from 5.1/5.2 — a clip should be identifiable as yours with the sound off.

6The Script System

The structure stays fixed while every script's words are original — which is exactly what keeps you on the right side of YouTube's inauthentic-content policy. Retention is won or lost at three moments: second 5, second 30, and the mid-video dip around minute 5. Everything in this section is aimed at those three moments.

6.1The shared long-form skeleton (10–14 min, ~1,600–2,200 words at 155 wpm)

TimeBeatJob

Writing rules (both channels): write for the ear — average sentence under 18 words; one idea per sentence; concrete numbers over adjectives ("a 70-ton box" beats "a massive box"); a visual change cue at least every 40 seconds; the word "you" early and often.

6.2The retention toolkit — seven devices

These are the moves that separate a script that reads well from one that holds. The prompt templates below instruct the AI to use them; the rewrite pass (6.5) checks that it actually did.

1. Open Loop. Tease specific unresolved information early; close loops in the order planted. "Three numbers decide whether you'll ever own a home. The third is the one nobody talks about."
2. Snapback. After any dense stretch, yank the viewer back to the stakes. "Remember that two-and-a-half-years figure? Watch what happens to it."
3. Concrete Anchor. Every number above a million becomes a physical image, marked for fact-check. "That's every home in the state of Ohio — bought twice over. [VERIFY]"
4. Pattern Interrupt. Every 60–90 seconds, change the sensory texture: music drop, a beat of silence, hard cut to black, a sudden direct question. Scripted via [MUSIC] / [SFX] cues — not left to the edit.
5. Rule of Three. Escalate evidence in threes; the third item breaks the pattern the first two set. "No homes. No water. And no farms — because farming hadn't been invented yet."
6. The So-What Test. Every sentence either advances the story or raises the stakes. If it does neither, it dies in the rewrite.
7. Transition stingers. One-line pivots between acts, rotated, never repeated within a script: "That's the what. Here's the why." · "Which leaves one question." · "Now watch what happens." · "Here's where it gets expensive." (finance) · "And that's when the story stops making sense." (mystery)

6.5The rewrite protocol — where quality actually comes from

First-draft AI scripts are structurally fine and verbally flat. The gap closes in two passes plus one prompt:

Pass A — structure (5 min): Does the hook contain a number and tension inside the first two sentences? Are two loops planted, and closed in order? Does the re-hook land between 5:00 and 6:00? Is every beat within ±20 seconds of the skeleton? Is there exactly one CTA?

Pass B — line edit (10 min): Read it aloud — rewrite anything you stumble on. Cut 10% of the words. Replace weak verbs (is/are/has/gets) where a strong verb exists. Make the first sentence of every act its strongest. Resolve every [VERIFY] and [NEED].

7Guardrails: Staying Monetized (and Legal)

Everything above only pays if the channels stay in the YouTube Partner Program, advertiser-friendly, and out of legal trouble. These nine rules are the insurance policy.

  • Beat the inauthentic-content policy by design: original scripts, a human edit pass on every video, one consistent branded voice and style, signature segments, sources cited on-screen. Never upload identical content across multiple channels; never ship a video no human watched end-to-end.
  • Synthetic-media disclosure: toggle YouTube's altered/synthetic-content flag whenever AI footage could be mistaken for real events or real people. Stylized painterly art doesn't need it; photorealistic "historical footage" does.
  • FTC disclosure: every description with affiliate links carries the disclosure line (5.5). Sponsored videos get the platform's paid-promotion flag AND a spoken/on-screen "#ad" — the FTC requires clear and conspicuous disclosure, not a buried link.
  • Voice & likeness: use platform-licensed synthetic voices only — never clone a real person's voice; never depict living private individuals photorealistically; public figures only in documented, newsworthy contexts.
  • Defamation discipline (the Dark Side bucket): named companies and living people get only the documented public record — court filings, regulator actions, major-outlet reporting — with attribution ("according to the SEC complaint…"). When in doubt, indict the mechanism, not the person.
  • Finance is YMYL: fact-check every number against a primary source (FRED, BLS, Census), keep the "not financial advice" line in every description, never promise returns.
  • Mystery framing: evidence-first, questions not cover-ups. The debunk videos in Section 3 are deliberate — they're the advertiser-friendliness insurance.
  • Licensing: only stock and licensed footage and music. Nothing ripped, ever — one strike cascade can end a channel.
  • Platform hygiene: native uploads, no cross-platform watermarks, US-skewed topics for the 3–5x RPM premium.

8Getting Started: The First 30 Days

The plan is complete. What kills new channels now isn't strategy — it's the cold start: overwhelm in week one, panic at week four's view counts, thrash at week six. This section is the anti-quit protocol.

8.1Launch sequence — stagger, don't parallel-launch

Reserve both brands' handles on day 1, but launch the content staggered: two simultaneous channel launches double the week-one workload exactly when every step is still unfamiliar. A two-week stagger costs nothing at day 90.

WeekMilestones
Week 1Finance only: setup (4.1), style lock (5), bank 6 scripts, publish video #1 (idea #1) plus its daily shorts.
Week 2Finance to 2–3 long-forms/week; bank mystery scripts during the Monday batch.
Week 3Launch mysteries with Göbekli Tepe and its shorts; both channels now run the 4.4 schedule.
Week 4Full pyramid operating. First analytics read — impressions and CTR only; ignore view counts (see 8.3).

8.2Naming the channels

  • Short, sayable, spellable after hearing it once. No numbers, underscores, or hyphens.
  • Hints the niche without locking it — abstract beats literal ("HousingFacts2026" ages badly; a metaphor doesn't).
  • Patterns that work: finance — objects of the trade (Ledger, Fine Print, Paper Trail, The Margin); mysteries — evocations of deep time (Before the Wheel, The Buried Age, Older Than Empires).
  • Elegant move: name the channel after its signature segment, so the brand and the format reinforce each other.
  • The same handle must be free on YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, and Pinterest (grab the .com if it's cheap). Check before falling in love; expect three or four attempts.
  • Passes the spoken test — it has to sound natural in the outro sentence.

8.3The cold-start reality — read this at week 4, and again at week 8

  • New channels get trickle impressions while YouTube tests them. The first 10–15 videos earning under 100 views each is normal, not a verdict.
  • Judgment day is video 15 / day 45 — not video 5. The 4.8 kill rules apply to formats after 8 attempts, never to a channel before day 90.
  • Never delete or re-upload early low-view videos. They're the library the algorithm mines later; deletion resets your data.
  • Never buy subscribers or engagement — dead subscribers permanently suppress the impressions your videos earn.
  • Expect one video to eventually do 10–50x the others. The outlier, not the average, is how channels break out. The job until then: keep the slots filled, hold the quality floor.

8.4The reactive calendar — the 30% is schedulable

Finance "news" runs on a published schedule. Build each reactive video's evergreen shell in advance, drop the numbers in on release day, publish within 24 hours:

  • CPI release (monthly, 8:30am ET) → "Why everything feels expensive" update angles.
  • Jobs report (first Friday, monthly) → labor-market and AI-and-jobs angles.
  • FOMC decision (eight per year) → Fed explainer re-cuts.
  • Case-Shiller / existing-home sales (monthly) → the Housing Market Update format (idea #10).

Mysteries equivalent: Google Alerts on "archaeological discovery," "ancient DNA," and each covered site. A fresh dig announcement is that channel's data drop — and a reason to sequel an existing video.

8.5The weekly metrics row (every Friday, one row)

  • Per video: impressions, CTR, average view duration %, views, top traffic source.
  • Per channel: subscribers, watch hours (YPP progress), shorts views.
  • One decision per week, maximum, drawn from the data ("number-led thumbnails beat object-only 2:1 — update the template"). Change more than one thing at a time and nothing is attributable.

8.6Ship at 80%

Video #1 will be slightly embarrassing. Publish it anyway. The system improves through reps against the checklist, not through polishing one artifact — quality rises with every video; it is never reached by delaying one. The hard floor stays the 6.7 checklist, every video, no exceptions.

If you remember only five things

  1. Long-form is the business. Shorts are the advertising.
  2. The human pass (6.7) is never skipped — it is the monetization moat.
  3. Evergreen compounds; news decays. Hold the 70/30 line.
  4. Signature segments in every video: The Ledger, The Ladder.
  5. Judge at video 15, not video 5. Kill formats, not channels, before day 90.
